## Types
Fixed pattern, every year 2021-2025 (and 2026):
- Section A: 30 MCQ (Q1-10 at 1 mark, Q11-30 at 2 marks), 4 options, negative marking 1/3 and 2/3. 50 marks.
- Section B: 10 MSQ at 2 marks, one or more correct, no negative, no partial. 20 marks.
- Section C: 20 NAT (Q41-50 at 1 mark, Q51-60 at 2 marks), real number, tolerance range in key, no negative. 30 marks.
Biology share by type (mean 2021-25): MCQ 1-mark ~70% bio, MCQ 2-mark ~80% bio, MSQ ~85% bio, NAT ~40% bio (NAT is where most maths/physics/chem sits).

What biology NAT questions compute (typical 6-9 per paper):
- Hardy-Weinberg: heterozygote or carrier frequency, allele frequency from genotype counts (every year).
- Linkage/crosses: map units to double crossover, test cross class %, gamete types, tetrahybrid proportion, number of genotypes.
- Enzyme kinetics: Km/Vmax at given fraction, competitive inhibition velocity, dG from Keq, coupled reaction dG, transport free energy against gradient.
- Molecular counting: peptide bonds, codons, bases from mol % (Chargaff), mRNA/protein length, UTR subtraction in cDNA, transcription time, GTP per step.
- Techniques: PCR product counting, restriction fragments/bands on plasmid map, agarose weight, probability of a restriction site, magnification, resolution, Beer-Lambert, dilution.
- Microbial growth: number of cells after n generations, doubling time, cells per volume.
- Ecology: Shannon index, capture-recapture estimate, population change from birth/death rates.

Style mix inside MCQ/MSQ: plain recall of a fact (~45%), "which is NOT/INCORRECT" (~15%), match the following with 4 pairs (1-2 per paper, 4 options of pairings), assertion-reason (0-1 per paper, 2023 and 2024), "which of the following is/are true" statements (all MSQ), experiment/data/figure based (pedigree, selection curve, gel/plasmid map, alga with bacteria, phylogenetic tree: 1-3 per paper; figures are images so text-only generator should describe them in words).

## Difficulty rubric
Scale for this exam (graduate-entry, 2-mark items are usually 3+):
1. Recall one fact or definition (organelle of ETC, hormone for ripening, ploidy of sporophyte).
2. Direct concept with one distinction or one-step reasoning (which cell type HIV infects, Ig crossing placenta, choose the right sigma factor).
3. Standard textbook application or two-step reasoning; typical "which is NOT correct" with 4 plausible options; match-the-following; one-formula NAT (HW, doubling, Chargaff, dG=-RTlnK).
4. Multi-concept or multi-step: data/figure interpretation, MSQ with 4 subtle statements, NAT needing 2-3 formulas or careful setup (map-unit to test-cross class, competitive inhibitor velocity, cDNA UTR subtraction, tetrahybrid proportion).
5. Novel integration, long chains or tricky traps: plasmid orientation + digestion fragments, carrier frequency under conditions, paraphyletic taxa in a tree, MSQ where one wrong option flips the answer and all four look right.

Observed mix of biology questions in real papers (my judgement, mean 2021-25): L1 20%, L2 30%, L3 28%, L4 17%, L5 5%. Paper-wise, 1-mark MCQ are L1-2; 2-mark MCQ L2-4; MSQ L3-5; NAT 1-mark L2-3; NAT 2-mark L3-5.
App mapping: Easy = mostly L1-2 (about 80% L1-2, rest 3); Medium = mostly L3 (about 60% L3, 20% L2, 20% L4); Hard = mostly L4-5 (about 75% L4-5, rest 3); Mixed = real mix above.

## Style and traps
1. Negation stems: "which is NOT / INCORRECT / ABSENT" with three true statements and one subtly false. Example: four statements about tundra and boreal biomes, one with an impossible temperature; or four statements on photosystems, one assigning water splitting to the wrong photosystem.
2. Look-alike pairs in matching: Group I organisms/drugs vs Group II mechanisms where two options swap neighbouring pairs. Example: four antibiotics matched to which ribosomal subunit or enzyme they hit (30S vs 50S vs 60S vs RNA polymerase beta subunit).
3. Prokaryote vs eukaryote swap: same process, different machinery. Example: ask the number of GTP used per translocation step in bacteria, or which sigma factor serves nitrogen assimilation versus stationary phase.
4. MSQ with all-or-nothing marking: every option is a genuine textbook claim; one hides a wrong qualifier (always, only, all). Example: statements on sterilization where filtration with a 0.22 micron membrane is said to remove viruses.
5. NAT unit and counting traps: count molecules not moles, double-stranded vs single-stranded, bp vs aa (subtract UTRs, divide by 3, minus stop codon), generations vs time. Example: a 614 bp cDNA with stated 5' and 3' UTR lengths, asked for amino acids.
6. Hardy-Weinberg wording: asked "frequency of carriers" vs "heterozygotes" vs "percent of carriers passing allele"; or population size 400 with 64 recessive, asked for heterozygote count (convert q^2 to q first).
7. Linkage trap: test cross of a coupling F1; the asked class (parental ddee) is (1 - map units)/2, not the map-unit fraction. Double crossover = product of the two recombination frequencies.
8. Technique questions use a map or gel: restriction map of a plasmid with two enzymes, how many bands visible; PCR forgotten primer gives linear (not exponential) single-stranded product; minimum cycles before the exact-length product appears (cycle 3).
9. Assertion-reason on cell/immune/physiology topics: both statements true but reason is not the explanation, or assertion true and reason false (double circulation, nitric oxide signalling, TEM resolving power).
10. Energetics from tables: bind affinity "sweetest" via most negative dG; coupled reactions add dG values; Keq to dG uses -RT ln K at 25 C; equilibrium constant given for the reverse reaction.
11. Shared general section: maths/physics/chem items appear with biological flavour (blood flow continuity, de Broglie of electron, photon energy, ideal gas); a generator for the biology part should keep these out unless the app asks for the general section.
