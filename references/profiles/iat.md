## Difficulty rubric
1 = single-fact recall straight from NCERT line (a term, a definition).
2 = recall plus one small link (name to function; a simple compare of two items).
3 = one-step reasoning on a known concept (predict direction of change, pick the true statement among close ones).
4 = two-step reasoning or a mini data set (inhibitor to ATP order, gamete ratios to parent genotypes, gel to site map).
5 = multi-step, needs NCERT concept plus outside-line extension or unusual setting (pedigree/quantitative with traps, concept from one chapter used in another).

## Style and traps
1. Exact-looking options differ by one word or one sign. Paraphrased example: four options on a hormone-feedback chain that differ only in whether each hormone goes up or down; the student must track all links.
2. Direction pairs: options are all combinations of high/low for two variables (e.g. acidity and temperature on oxygen release). Know each variable independently.
3. Negative stems (FALSE / INCORRECT) with three true statements; one wrong number or one wrong organ is the trap (e.g. "most solar energy is captured by plants" type exaggeration).
4. Near-fact distractors from the same NCERT table: a wrong gamete type (haploid vs diploid origin), a wrong chromosome-region name, lookalike terms (centromere / centrosome / centriole / telomere).
5. Quantitative-qualitative hybrids: no calculation needed, but ordering of outcomes (A < B = C) must be reasoned from a blocked pathway; trap is thinking both blocks stop the whole chain equally.
6. Genetics set in an unusual organism (haplodiploid insects, linked genes from gamete frequencies): rules from NCERT but applied to a fresh case; check who can have a father, which cells divide by mitosis.
7. Codon / anticodon direction trap: anticodon is read 5' to 3' and pairs antiparallel; options list the matching codon so a student who forgets antiparallel pairing picks a decoy.
8. Experimental-design items: restriction digest, inhibitor treatments, untreated control. Always include the control in the ranking.
9. Match-the-column with 4 rows and combos that share two correct pairs, so a half-known list still leaves two options.
10. Plain conceptual recall is a gift question (one per paper or two); keep distractors from the same category, never silly.

[public] Typical IAT stems use NCERT terms exactly, but the logic goes beyond rote; option sets are short.
